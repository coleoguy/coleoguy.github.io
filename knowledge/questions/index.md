# Open research questions

Hypotheses the lab thinks are worth testing. Each is generated either from the literature synthesis overnight job or by Heath directly.

## Proposed

1. The ancestral scarab karyotype reconstructed by RACA will fall at or near 2n = 20 (the modal number in Scarabaeidae sensu stricto), and derived lineages with substantially higher 2n will show a net excess of fissions over fusions concentrated on autosomes rather than sex-linked elements — opposite to the fusion excess expected in lineages showing progressive sex chromosome heteromorphism. This predicts that autosomal fission and sex-chromosomal fusion are partially decoupled evolutionary processes operating on different timescales within the same clade.
   _proposed: 2026-09-27; project: `p_028`; novelty: 0.7; feasibility: 0.8_

2. Within Scarabaeidae, lineages that have undergone fusion-based karyotype reduction (low 2n) will show elevated transposable element (TE) density at synteny breakpoints relative to genomic background, and this enrichment will be stronger in lineages with heteromorphic sex chromosomes than in those with homomorphic or absent sex chromosomes. The mechanistic prediction is that TE-mediated illegitimate recombination is a proximate driver of the fusions concentrated in heteromorphic lineages, rather than fusions being purely drift-driven events that happen to co-occur with sex chromosome differentiation.
   _proposed: 2026-09-27; project: `p_028`; novelty: 0.7; feasibility: 0.8; based on: 10.1038/s41576-026-01012-7_

3. Achiasmy should be phylogenetically over-represented in taxa where PRDM9-directed recombination is absent or secondarily lost, because lineages lacking PRDM9-based hotspot specification already experience altered crossover placement and possibly reduced crossover number, lowering the evolutionary cost of transitioning to zero crossovers in one sex. Conversely, lineages retaining functional PRDM9 should show fewer independent origins of achiasmy per unit of phylogenetic diversity, because active hotspot regulation provides a more granular toolkit for sex-asymmetric recombination suppression short of complete achiasmy.
   _proposed: 2026-09-13; project: `p_002`; novelty: 0.7; feasibility: 0.6; based on: 10.1038/s41580-026-01020-9_

4. If achiasmy in the heterogametic sex can arise via long-range linkage dynamics rather than requiring prior chromosomal inversions, then lineages with young, undifferentiated sex chromosomes should show a higher-than-expected rate of achiasmy transitions relative to the prediction that achiasmy follows (rather than precedes or co-occurs with) inversion-based recombination suppression. Specifically, the phylogenetic ordering of achiasmy transitions and sex chromosome differentiation should show a non-trivial fraction of cases where achiasmy appears in taxa with low or absent sex-chromosome heteromorphism, contradicting the canonical 'inversions-first' model embedded in the project's current hypothesis.
   _proposed: 2026-09-13; project: `p_002`; novelty: 0.7; feasibility: 0.7; based on: 10.64898/2026.08.31.748364_

5. Environmental fluctuation rate interacts with epistasis sign to produce a crossover in optimal chromosome number: at intermediate fluctuation rates (neither fully stable nor maximally variable), positive and negative epistasis regimes converge on similar optima, whereas at the extremes they diverge maximally. This non-monotone relationship between fluctuation rate and the epistasis-regime divergence in optimal chromosome number implies that the selective pressure maintaining intermediate chromosome numbers is strongest at intermediate environmental variance.
   _proposed: 2026-05-31; project: `p_013`; novelty: 0.8; feasibility: 0.9; based on: 10.1371/journal.pgen.0030105_

6. The fitness cost of deviating from the optimal chromosome number is asymmetric: adding chromosomes beyond the optimum is more costly than removing an equivalent number, because supernumerary chromosomes impose multiplicative segregation error costs that scale with chromosome count, whereas fusions reduce recombination opportunity costs that scale more slowly under negative epistasis. This asymmetry should be largest in parameter cells combining strong negative epistasis with high environmental stability, and should shrink or reverse under positive epistasis.
   _proposed: 2026-05-31; project: `p_013`; novelty: 0.7; feasibility: 0.9; based on: 10.1093/genetics/157.2.745_

7. Achiasmy transitions in the heterogametic sex should be significantly over-represented in clades that have undergone autosome–sex chromosome fusions, because such fusions abruptly extend the non-recombining sex-linked region to previously autosomal loci and thereby intensify selection to suppress crossing-over across the expanded sex chromosome — producing a tighter temporal coupling between fusion events and the origin of achiasmy than would be expected under a model where achiasmy accumulates gradually with sex chromosome age. Concretely, the predicted waiting time from an autosome–sex chromosome fusion to acquisition of achiasmy should be shorter than the waiting time in lineages with equally old sex chromosomes that lack a recent fusion.
   _proposed: 2026-05-31; project: `p_002`; novelty: 0.8; feasibility: 0.6; based on: 10.1046/j.1420-9101.2003.00597.x_

8. If the primary selective pressure maintaining achiasmy is protection of sex chromosome linkage groups, then achiasmy in the heterogametic sex should be phylogenetically associated with older, more differentiated sex chromosomes (greater X–Y or Z–W divergence), whereas the minority of cases where the *homogametic* sex is achiasmatic should show no such association — instead predicting correlation with life-history or genome-structural predictors (e.g., genome size, holocentricity, or Ne proxies). Specifically, homogametic achiasmy is predicted to be randomly distributed with respect to sex chromosome age but non-randomly distributed with respect to karyotypic features that modulate recombination cost genome-wide.
   _proposed: 2026-05-31; project: `p_002`; novelty: 0.7; feasibility: 0.8; based on: 10.1046/j.1420-9101.2003.00597.x_

9. Environmental fluctuation rate and epistasis sign interact non-additively to determine optimal chromosome number: in fluctuating environments, the fitness advantage of higher chromosome number (predicted by the current hypothesis) is attenuated or eliminated under strong negative epistasis but is amplified under positive epistasis, producing a crossing interaction rather than parallel shifts. The mechanism is that frequent environmental change favors the increased recombinational mixing that higher chromosome number provides, but negative epistasis simultaneously penalizes that mixing, creating countervailing selection pressures that partially cancel.
   _proposed: 2026-05-10; project: `p_013`; novelty: 0.8; feasibility: 0.9; based on: 10.5281/zenodo.19959420_

10. The fitness cost of supernumerary chromosomes (chromosome number above the epistasis-specific optimum) is asymmetric with respect to epistasis sign: under negative epistasis, excess chromosomes impose a steeper per-unit fitness penalty than equivalent deficits, whereas under positive epistasis the penalty curve is shallower on the high-number side. This asymmetry arises because negative epistasis penalizes the increased recombinational disruption of co-adapted allele combinations that fragmentation across additional chromosomes produces, while positive epistasis is more tolerant of that disruption.
   _proposed: 2026-05-10; project: `p_013`; novelty: 0.7; feasibility: 0.9; based on: 10.1093/g3journal/jkag121_

11. If the heterogametic-sex bias in achiasmy arises primarily from selection to suppress sex-chromosome recombination (as assumed in Haldane 1922 and formalized in Burt et al. 1991), then in taxa with female heterogamety (ZW systems), achiasmy should occur preferentially in females, and the phylogenetic concentration of female achiasmy should track ZW sex chromosome differentiation age in the same quantitative way that male achiasmy tracks XY differentiation age in XY systems. A departure — specifically, a weaker association between ZW chromosome age and female achiasmy than between XY age and male achiasmy — would suggest an asymmetric evolutionary dynamic driven by a factor that differs between ZW and XY systems, such as the faster-Z effect or differences in the intensity of sexually antagonistic selection.
   _proposed: 2026-05-10; project: `p_002`; novelty: 0.7; feasibility: 0.7; based on: 10.64898/2026.04.27.721057_

12. Achiasmy is disproportionately retained — not merely acquired — in lineages that have undergone autosome–sex chromosome fusions, because such fusions extend the region requiring recombination suppression beyond what partial dosage compensation or inversion-based suppression can efficiently achieve. Specifically, taxa with neo-sex chromosomes formed by autosomal fusions should show a lower rate of secondary reversion from achiasmy to chiasmy than taxa with ancestrally differentiated sex chromosomes of comparable age, after controlling for phylogenetic inertia.
   _proposed: 2026-05-10; project: `p_002`; novelty: 0.7; feasibility: 0.7; based on: 10.64898/2026.04.27.721057_

13. The project hypothesis predicts achiasmy is enriched in the heterogametic sex as a downstream consequence of sex-chromosome selection. A complementary prediction is that *holokinetic* chromosome systems — where crossover placement is decoupled from centromere function — should show elevated rates of achiasmy evolution independent of sex-chromosome differentiation, because the primary mechanical constraint against achiasmy in monokinetic systems (obligate chiasma for metaphase I alignment) is relaxed. If so, the excess of achiasmy in heterogametic sexes should be substantially attenuated within holokinetic clades after controlling for karyotype structure.
   _proposed: 2026-05-03; project: `p_002`; novelty: 0.7; feasibility: 0.7; based on: 10.1093/biolre/ioag092_

14. If achiasmy evolves preferentially as a mechanism to suppress sex-chromosome recombination, then independent origins of achiasmy in the heterogametic sex should be enriched in lineages with older, more differentiated sex chromosomes, but the molecular route to achiasmy (loss vs. functional reversal of pro-crossover machinery) need not be conserved. Specifically, lineages in which RAD51-paralog function has already diverged from the ancestral pro-crossover role — as documented for XRCC2 across angiosperms — may face a lower mutational barrier to evolving achiasmy and should therefore show elevated rates of achiasmate transition per unit phylogenetic time relative to lineages retaining ancestral crossover machinery.
   _proposed: 2026-05-03; project: `p_002`; novelty: 0.7; feasibility: 0.5; based on: 10.1093/plphys/kiag255_
