# Open research questions

Hypotheses the lab thinks are worth testing. Each is generated either from the literature synthesis overnight job or by Heath directly.

## Proposed

1. The project hypothesis predicts achiasmy is enriched in the heterogametic sex as a downstream consequence of sex-chromosome selection. A complementary prediction is that *holokinetic* chromosome systems — where crossover placement is decoupled from centromere function — should show elevated rates of achiasmy evolution independent of sex-chromosome differentiation, because the primary mechanical constraint against achiasmy in monokinetic systems (obligate chiasma for metaphase I alignment) is relaxed. If so, the excess of achiasmy in heterogametic sexes should be substantially attenuated within holokinetic clades after controlling for karyotype structure.
   _proposed: 2026-05-03; project: `p_002`; novelty: 0.7; feasibility: 0.7; based on: 10.1093/biolre/ioag092_

2. If achiasmy evolves preferentially as a mechanism to suppress sex-chromosome recombination, then independent origins of achiasmy in the heterogametic sex should be enriched in lineages with older, more differentiated sex chromosomes, but the molecular route to achiasmy (loss vs. functional reversal of pro-crossover machinery) need not be conserved. Specifically, lineages in which RAD51-paralog function has already diverged from the ancestral pro-crossover role — as documented for XRCC2 across angiosperms — may face a lower mutational barrier to evolving achiasmy and should therefore show elevated rates of achiasmate transition per unit phylogenetic time relative to lineages retaining ancestral crossover machinery.
   _proposed: 2026-05-03; project: `p_002`; novelty: 0.7; feasibility: 0.5; based on: 10.1093/plphys/kiag255_
