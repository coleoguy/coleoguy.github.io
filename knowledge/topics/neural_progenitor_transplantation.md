# Neural Progenitor Transplantation

## Current understanding

Neural progenitor cell (NPC) transplantation has emerged as a candidate strategy for repairing the injured spinal cord, with the hope that graft-derived neurons could integrate into host motor circuits and thereby support functional recovery. A critical and still-unresolved question is *how* those grafted neurons wire into host circuitry — whether they form direct (monosynaptic) connections with motor neurons, or whether any functional integration is mediated primarily through multi-step, polysynaptic relays.

Quantitative viral tracing in a mouse contusion model provides an initial answer: both modes of connectivity exist, but they are not equivalent. Monosynaptic input from host lumbar motor neurons to graft neurons — measured by retrograde rabies virus restricted to motor-neuron starter cells — is sparse. The rabies infectivity index (graft neurons labeled per primary motor-neuron starter) is approximately 1.59, indicating that each motor neuron contacts on average only about 1–2 graft-derived cells through a single synaptic step ([2025 circuitry injured spine, Finding 1](/knowledge/papers/2025_circuitry_injured_spine/#finding-1)).

By contrast, polysynaptic connectivity — assessed with pseudorabies virus (PRV), which propagates trans-synaptically across multiple steps — is roughly four times greater, yielding an infectivity index of ~6.39 ([2025 circuitry injured spine, Finding 2](/knowledge/papers/2025_circuitry_injured_spine/#finding-2)). This four-fold difference indicates that graft neurons embed themselves predominantly within multisynaptic relay networks rather than as direct partners of motor neurons. Mechanistically, this suggests that any locomotor benefit from NPC grafts is more likely mediated through circuit-level modulation or indirect relays than through classic monosynaptic drive.

These numbers represent an early quantitative scaffold for the field. They do not yet speak to whether the observed connectivity is sufficient for functional recovery, whether it changes over longer post-transplant intervals, or whether different graft preparations or injury severities would shift the balance between mono- and polysynaptic integration.

## Supporting evidence

- [2025 circuitry injured spine, Finding 1](/knowledge/papers/2025_circuitry_injured_spine/#finding-1) — Retrograde rabies tracing from host lumbar motor neurons yields a graft infectivity index of ~1.59 ± 0.55, quantifying sparse direct synaptic connectivity from motor neurons onto graft-derived cells.

- [2025 circuitry injured spine, Finding 2](/knowledge/papers/2025_circuitry_injured_spine/#finding-2) — PRV-based polysynaptic tracing yields an infectivity index of ~6.39 ± 1.43, approximately four times the monosynaptic value, indicating that graft neurons are preferentially integrated into hindlimb motor circuits via multisynaptic relays.

## Contradictions / open disagreements

No directly contradicting findings are present in the current evidence base for this page. However, several methodological tensions deserve note:

- The rabies tracing approach uses motor-neuron-restricted starter cells and depends on helper virus expression efficiency; variability in these parameters could cause underestimation of true monosynaptic connectivity.
- PRV infectivity at a 72-hour endpoint is sensitive to replication kinetics and injection route, potentially inflating or biasing estimates of polysynaptic connectivity relative to functionally meaningful synapses.
- Both findings come from a single contusion model in mice at fixed timepoints. Whether the ~4:1 polysynaptic-to-monosynaptic ratio is a general feature of NPC graft integration, or specific to this model system, remains to be tested.

## Tealc's citation-neighborhood suggestions

- Studies using rabies-based circuit mapping in other spinal interneuron transplantation paradigms (e.g., inhibitory progenitor grafts for pain or spasticity) would provide comparative connectivity benchmarks.
- Electrophysiological characterization of identified graft-to-host synapses (e.g., patch-clamp in slice) would help validate whether virally traced connections are functionally active.
- Longitudinal tracing studies at multiple post-transplant timepoints would clarify whether connectivity matures or remodels over time.
