# Karyotype Evolution

## Current understanding

Karyotype structure — the number, size, and organization of chromosomes within a genome — varies considerably across the tree of life, yet certain lineages show remarkable conservation over deep evolutionary time. In beetles (Coleoptera), the ancestral karyotype of 2n=20 (9 autosomes + XY sex chromosomes) is exceptionally widespread, persisting across highly diverse families and subfamilies. This modal karyotype represents one of the clearest examples of conserved chromosomal architecture in insects.

New chromosome-level genome assemblies are proving instrumental in confirming and extending cytogenetic knowledge, particularly for rare or poorly studied taxa. Hi-C scaffolding can recover major chromosomal units in the absence of direct cytogenetic counts, allowing karyotype inference at genomic resolution.

## Supporting evidence

The chromosome-level assembly of the endangered long-armed scarab *Cheirotonus formosanus* (Scarabaeidae: Euchirinae) offers a concrete genomic anchor for the ancestral beetle karyotype. Hi-C contact mapping yielded 10 primary large scaffolds — 9 putative autosomes plus an X chromosome — directly consistent with the 2n=20 (9AA+XY) modal karyotype documented across the majority of Coleoptera. As the authors note, "The final corrected contact map displayed 10 primary large scaffolds, including 9 autosomes and X chromosomes. This genetic architecture is highly consistent with known cytogenetic data for the group. The majority of Coleoptera possess a diploid number of 2n = 20 (9AA + XY)." This finding supports the view that karyotype structure within Euchirinae — and more broadly across Scarabaeidae — has remained conserved relative to the deep coleopteran ancestor. See [Chromosome-Level Reference Genome of an Endemic, Endangered Long-Armed Scarab (<i>Cheirotonus formosanus</i>): Discovery of a Putative Y-Linked Scaffold and Demographic History., Finding 1](/knowledge/papers/10_1002_ece3_73483/#finding-1) for details.

## Contradictions / open disagreements

The karyotype assignment for *C. formosanus* rests on genomic scaffold counts and Hi-C contact patterns rather than on direct cytogenetic preparation from this species. No new karyotype counts were generated; the 2n=20 inference is supported by consistency with related taxa rather than independent evidence. This methodological gap means the claim, while plausible, has not been confirmed by classical cytogenetics for this particular species. Broader taxonomic sampling with paired cytogenetic and genomic data would strengthen confidence in the conserved-karyotype narrative.

## Tealc's citation-neighborhood suggestions

Studies systematically surveying chromosome number variation across Coleoptera (e.g., large-scale cytogenetic databases or comparative genomic analyses of beetle sex chromosome evolution) would complement the single-species anchor provided here and help quantify the true frequency and stability of the 2n=20 karyotype across the order.
