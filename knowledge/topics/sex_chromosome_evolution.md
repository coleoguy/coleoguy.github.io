# Sex Chromosome Evolution

## Current understanding

Sex chromosomes evolve from ordinary autosomes through a process heavily shaped by sexual antagonism — the tension between alleles that benefit one sex at a cost to the other. A key mechanism involves chromosomal inversions that suppress recombination between the sex-determining region (SDR) and nearby sexually antagonistic (SA) loci, allowing sex-specific alleles to co-segregate. Blackmon Lab modeling work shows that this short-term resolution of sexual conflict carries long-term consequences: inversions linking male-beneficial alleles to the Y chromosome can spread through a population even when they impose meaningful fitness costs via aneuploidy, setting the Y on a path toward degeneration — the core of the "fragile Y" thesis.

Quantitatively, [Long-Term Fragility of Y Chromosomes Is Dominated by Short-Term Resolution of Sexual Antagonism., Finding 1](/knowledge/papers/10_1534_genetics_117_300382/#finding-1) shows that Y chromosome inversions can fix when they increase aneuploidy by approximately 4–6%, as long as the captured male-beneficial allele is dominant and selection is at least s ≈ 0.2. The dynamics differ substantially between X and Y chromosomes and depend on the dominance architecture of the SA allele: when the male-beneficial allele is recessive (h < ~0.3), X chromosome inversions are instead maintained as stable polymorphisms rather than fixing ([Finding 2](/knowledge/papers/10_1534_genetics_117_300382/#finding-2)). Higher background recombination between the SDR and the SA locus permits inversions with larger aneuploidy costs to fix, an effect more pronounced on the Y than on the X ([Finding 3](/knowledge/papers/10_1534_genetics_117_300382/#finding-3)).

A complementary line of theory extends this framework to *achiasmy* — the complete elimination of crossovers in one sex, observed most commonly in males. [Drivers of achiasmatic meiosis: sexual antagonism versus heteromorphy-dependent aneuploidy across sex-chromosome divergence., Finding 1](/knowledge/papers/10_1093_g3journal_jkaf217/#finding-1) shows that achiasmy-causing mutations fix most readily on the Y chromosome under sexual antagonism, reaching equilibrium ~4× faster than on the X and ~18× faster than on autosomes. Crucially, the selective pressure driving achiasmy is not static: [Finding 2](/knowledge/papers/10_1093_g3journal_jkaf217/#finding-2) demonstrates that sexual antagonism dominates in young, homomorphic sex-chromosome systems, while heteromorphy-dependent aneuploidy becomes the primary force in highly diverged, heteromorphic systems. This provides a unified explanation for why different taxa may evolve achiasmy for different proximate reasons depending on where they sit on the continuum of sex-chromosome divergence. An unexpected prediction emerges under the heteromorphy-dependent aneuploidy regime: achiasmy mutations can invade across a *broader* parameter space on autosomes than on X chromosomes — despite the Y being most permissive overall — because autosomes reside in males half the time versus only one-third of the time for X chromosomes ([Finding 3](/knowledge/papers/10_1093_g3journal_jkaf217/#finding-3)).

These theoretical predictions become especially salient in Coleoptera, where Y chromosomes are notoriously punctiform and difficult to characterize. A chromosome-level genome assembly of the endangered long-armed scarab *Cheirotonus formosanus* provides one of the first empirical glimpses of a beetle Y. [Chromosome-Level Reference Genome of an Endemic, Endangered Long-Armed Scarab (*Cheirotonus formosanus*): Discovery of a Putative Y-Linked Scaffold and Demographic History., Finding 1](/knowledge/papers/10_1002_ece3_73483/#finding-1) identified a 1.1 Mbp scaffold with female:male read-depth ratios near 0 and male coverage averaging ~0.5× of autosomal depth — the signature of hemizygous, single-copy Y sequence. Its compact size is consistent with the gene-poor, degenerate Y predicted by the fragile-Y framework. On this scaffold, [Finding 2](/knowledge/papers/10_1002_ece3_73483/#finding-2) reports a gene model with JARID1/KDM5 family architecture present in male reads but entirely absent from female data, suggesting convergent sex-linkage of a KDM5-like demethylase — a parallel to *KDM5D* on the mammalian Y chromosome.

## Supporting evidence

- [Long-Term Fragility of Y Chromosomes Is Dominated by Short-Term Resolution of Sexual Antagonism., Finding 1](/knowledge/papers/10_1534_genetics_117_300382/#finding-1): Y inversions tolerate 4–6% aneuploidy cost when the male-beneficial allele is dominant and s ≥ 0.2.
- [Long-Term Fragility of Y Chromosomes Is Dominated by Short-Term Resolution of Sexual Antagonism., Finding 2](/knowledge/papers/10_1534_genetics_117_300382/#finding-2): Recessive male-beneficial alleles (h < ~0.3) lead to stable polymorphism rather than fixation of X chromosome inversions.
- [Long-Term Fragility of Y Chromosomes Is Dominated by Short-Term Resolution of Sexual Antagonism., Finding 3](/knowledge/papers/10_1534_genetics_117_300382/#finding-3): Higher SDR–SA recombination rates favor fixation of larger-cost inversions, especially on the Y; the benefit saturates for X inversions above r > 0.3.
- [Drivers of achiasmatic meiosis: sexual antagonism versus heteromorphy-dependent aneuploidy across sex-chromosome divergence., Finding 1](/knowledge/papers/10_1093_g3journal_jkaf217/#finding-1): Achiasmy-causing mutations fix ~4× faster on the Y than on the X and ~18× faster than on autosomes under sexual antagonism.
- [Drivers of achiasmatic meiosis: sexual antagonism versus heteromorphy-dependent aneuploidy across sex-chromosome divergence., Finding 2](/knowledge/papers/10_1093_g3journal_jkaf217/#finding-2): The dominant selective driver of achiasmy shifts from sexual antagonism (young/homomorphic) to heteromorphy-dependent aneuploidy (highly diverged/heteromorphic) across sex-chromosome evolution.
- [Drivers of achiasmatic meiosis: sexual antagonism versus heteromorphy-dependent aneuploidy across sex-chromosome divergence., Finding 3](/knowledge/papers/10_1093_g3journal_jkaf217/#finding-3): Under aneuploidy selection, autosomes permit broader invasion of achiasmy mutations than X chromosomes due to greater male residence time.
- [Chromosome-Level Reference Genome of an Endemic, Endangered Long-Armed Scarab (*Cheirotonus formosanus*): Discovery of a Putative Y-Linked Scaffold and Demographic History., Finding 1](/knowledge/papers/10_1002_ece3_73483/#finding-1): A 1.1 Mbp putative Y-linked scaffold in *C. formosanus* shows ~0.5× male coverage and near-zero female:male depth ratios, consistent with hemizygous Y sequence in a beetle.
- [Chromosome-Level Reference Genome of an Endemic, Endangered Long-Armed Scarab (*Cheirotonus formosanus*): Discovery of a Putative Y-Linked Scaffold and Demographic History., Finding 2](/knowledge/papers/10_1002_ece3_73483/#finding-2): A KDM5-like demethylase gene model on the putative Y scaffold is covered by male reads and absent in female data, paralleling *KDM5D* sex-linkage in mammals.

## Contradictions / open disagreements

The inversion-fixation model and the achiasmy model share a common framework but differ in scope and assumptions. The inversion model uses a three-locus deterministic system with multiplicative aneuploidy costs and no drift; the achiasmy model relies on functional forms for SA benefits and aneuploidy costs that the authors acknowledge are not empirically pinned down. The quantitative thresholds from both models (s ≈ 0.2, h ≈ 0.3, r > 0.3; ~4× and ~18× rate differences) should therefore be treated as illustrative rather than universal, and may not transfer to finite populations or alternative dominance/linkage regimes.

The two hypotheses for achiasmy — sexual antagonism vs. heteromorphy-dependent aneuploidy — are presented as sequentially dominant across a divergence continuum, but the model does not empirically validate this transition or specify where on that continuum any given lineage falls.

The *C. formosanus* Y-scaffold identification rests on read-depth ratios from a single male and single female individual, without PCR confirmation or population-level validation. Some of the 1.1 Mbp scaffold could reflect repeat artifacts or male-biased heterochromatic sequence rather than a structurally coherent Y chromosome. Similarly, the KDM5-like gene annotation is based on InterProScan domain architecture rather than orthology-based naming and lacks experimental validation, leaving open whether it is a true KDM5 ortholog or a paralog coincidentally enriched on male-specific sequence.

## Tealc's citation-neighborhood suggestions

The field would benefit from empirical studies directly measuring aneuploidy rates as a function of PAR length and sex-chromosome heteromorphy across diverse taxa, as well as population-genetic estimates of selection coefficients for SA alleles linked to sex chromosomes. Studies on achiasmy distribution and Y chromosome turnover rates in groups with rapid sex chromosome evolution (e.g., Coleoptera, fish, Lepidoptera) could provide the lineage-specific calibration that both models currently lack. Functional work identifying achiasmy-causing loci and their chromosomal locations would directly test the residence-time prediction. For Coleoptera specifically, multi-individual re-sequencing and long-read assembly would help validate the *C. formosanus* Y scaffold and ground-truth the chromosome-quotient method in beetles.
