# 2025 circuitry injured spine

## Summary

_Ingested 2026-04-21. 2 findings extracted and verified._

## Findings worth citing

<a id="finding-1"></a>
### Finding 1 — Spontaneous monosynaptic connectivity between graft-derived neurons and host lumbar motor neurons is low, with a rabies infectivity index of approximately 1.59 from host to graft.

> By normalizing the numbers of rabies+ graft neurons to primarily infected rabies+ motor neurons, we found that the infectivity index of rabies from host to graft is ~1.59 ± 0.55
— p. 2

*Why this is citable:* Provides a quantitative benchmark for how sparsely NPC graft neurons form direct synapses with host motor neurons, a key metric for evaluating the mechanistic basis of NPC-based SCI therapies.

*Counter / limitation:* Measured in a single mouse contusion model at a fixed timepoint using rabies tracing, which may underestimate connectivity due to variable helper virus expression and motor-neuron-restricted starter cells.

<a id="finding-2"></a>
### Finding 2 — Polysynaptic connectivity from graft neurons into hindlimb motor circuits is roughly four times greater than monosynaptic connectivity, with a PRV infectivity index of ~6.39.

> we found that the infectivity index of PR from host to graft is ~6.39 ± 1.43 (Fig. 2j); 4 times higher than the rabies infectivity index
— p. 2

*Why this is citable:* Establishes that graft-derived neurons integrate predominantly via polysynaptic relays rather than direct connections, informing mechanistic models of how NPC grafts could support motor recovery.

*Counter / limitation:* PRV spread depends on replication kinetics and survival time; the 72-hour endpoint and injection route may inflate or bias apparent polysynaptic connectivity relative to functionally meaningful synapses.
