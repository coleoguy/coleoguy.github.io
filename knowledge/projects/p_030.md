# Chromosome Selection

is there a specific number of chromosomes that is most fit given levels and types of epistasis and environmental stability

<!-- tealc:project-status-start -->

**Status:** active

**Current hypothesis:**

The number of chromosomes an organism carries affects adaptive fitness in fluctuating environments because more chromosomes produce more obligate crossovers per meiosis, accelerating the breakdown of linkage disequilibrium and enabling faster reassembly of favorable multi-locus genotypes; this fitness advantage is predicted to be conditional on populations being variation-limited and modulated by the type and level of epistasis.

**Next action:**

Literature synthesis: identify whether anyone has derived an optimal chromosome number as a function of trait polygenicity (L causal loci) and selection regime. Key targets: Dapper & Payseur 2017 (Phil Trans B) recombination-rate optimum review; Veller et al. 2019/2020 obligate-chiasma + crossover-count theory; Otto & Barton modifier theory; Negm & Veller 2026 long-range LD under polygenic stabilizing selection; Charlesworth's work on Hill-Robertson with many loci. Output: 1-page brief naming the gap between continuous-recombination-rate theory and discrete-chromosome-number theory, plus 3-5 specific papers Mallory should read first when the project resumes next semester.
