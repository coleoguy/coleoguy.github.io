# Information capacity of finite populations (constant env)

Theoretical question: given N births per generation and Ne individuals contributing to the next generation in a CONSTANT environment, what is the maximum amount of heritable information (loci x alleles, or equivalently genetic variance maintained against drift) that selection can preserve with fidelity? Sits at the intersection of mutation-selection-drift balance, error threshold theory (Eigen), drift load, and the Felsenstein/Kimura/Charlesworth limits on the number of loci under simultaneous selection. Distinct from p_030 (which asks about chromosome number / recombination given polygenicity) — this asks about the upstream limit on how polygenic a trait can even be in a finite population.

<!-- tealc:project-status-start -->

**Status:** active

**Current hypothesis:**

There exists a finite upper bound on the number of independently-selected loci that a population of effective size Ne can maintain at non-equilibrium frequencies in a constant environment, set jointly by per-locus selection coefficient s, total mutation rate U, and Ne. The bound is conjectured to scale as L_max ~ Ne·s/U for additive selection in the strong-selection-weak-mutation regime, but the literature treatment is fragmentary across mutation-load, error-threshold, and substitution-load frameworks rather than unified.

**Next action:**

Literature synthesis: does anyone derive a unified expression for the maximum heritable information a finite population can maintain in a constant environment, given (N births, Ne reproducing)? Targets: Eigen 1971 error threshold; Kimura 1961 substitution load; Haldane 1957 cost-of-natural-selection; Felsenstein 1974 recombination & info maintenance; Bürger 2000 mathematical theory of selection-mutation-drift; Charlesworth on Hill-Robertson with many loci; recent work on polygenic adaptation limits in finite populations (Hayward & Sella, Simons et al.). Output: 1-page brief naming whether a closed-form bound exists, what assumptions it requires, and where Heath's question (constant environment, finite N births and Ne reproducing) fits or extends the existing framing.
