Verification evidence for the Google.org AI for Science application.

This page exists to make every claim in our application directly verifiable. Every artifact below is reachable in one click. The page is not linked from elsewhere on the site; the URL appears only in the application itself.

For reviewers: Each section names a claim made in the application and the public artifacts that back it. If something is not as you expect, please reach out to blackmon@tamu.edu.

1. Tealc is running today (not "we will build")

Application claim

Tealc is an autonomous lab member running 49 scheduled jobs on a 15-minute executive loop, drafting analyses overnight under an adversarial Opus 4.7 critic, with every tool call public.

Verify
  • Live console (rolling tool-call feed) — visit during US business hours to see active runs.
  • github.com/coleoguy/tealc — source code, MIT license, README, architecture documentation.
  • Full Tealc description — architecture, scaffolding, rigor layer, falsifiability instrument.
  • "Build Your Own Tealc" guidance — how an external researcher deploys a Tealc instance.

2. TraitTrawler is validated across kingdoms

Application claim

Multi-agent extractor with mandatory deterministic verbatim-quote validation. 6,059 records / 4,959 species (Coleoptera, 91.7% HAC and 96.2% SCS agreement vs. a hand-curated benchmark); 1,960 records across 7 kingdoms (circadian tau); zero core-architecture changes between domains.

Verify
  • github.com/coleoguy/TraitTrawler — open-source code, MIT license, full validation pipeline.
  • TraitTrawler overview page — current week's triage and extraction summary.
  • Cross-kingdom tau database — interactive web resource, 1,960 records, 175 species, 7 kingdoms.
  • Coleoptera karyotype database — public benchmark dataset.
  • Methods paper bioRxiv preprint: link to be added on submission.

3. The CURE produces publishable science

Application claim

Spring 2026: 51 undergraduate researchers, 36 public posters at the annual undergraduate research session, 30 manuscripts in preparation targeted for November 2026 submission. Fall 2025 cohort produced Copeland & Blackmon, "Chromosome number evolves at rates spanning seven orders of magnitude across eukaryotes," submitted to Science.

Verify
  • Biology & AI CURE page — Spring 2026 cohort, 51 student projects, methods stack.
  • Copeland & Blackmon bioRxiv preprint — full Science-targeted manuscript, 63,682 karyotypes across 55 monophyletic eukaryotic clades.
  • Spring 2026 cohort posters — every poster from the public undergraduate research session.
  • Lab undergraduate publication record — 8 first-author + 6 co-author papers across 12 undergraduates.
