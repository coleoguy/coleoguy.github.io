Lab OS: the work, in one place

The PI works from a private status monitor. Projects, people, commitments, and decisions can be reviewed there, with details available when they are needed. A status update becomes part of the project's shared context, so subsequent work begins from the latest recorded decision.

Behind the monitor, a chief-of-staff role coordinates research, student support, and grants and administration. The roles share project memory and maintain task ownership and completion records. Tealc provides the execution framework: scheduled jobs, tools, and the underlying records.

The monitor is a private working surface. This page describes its design; live tasks, student records, email, and project status remain private.

The PI workflow

Start with what needs a decision, then open a project or person for context. Update the status, clarify the next action, or record what was completed. The system should make the next useful step easier to find.

FocusReview one area at a timeProjects, people, and commitments have their own views.

Research, mentoring, teaching, administration, service, and grants create different kinds of work. Separate views keep those areas easy to find. Summaries lead to details, and the PI can update a record without working through an agent conversation first.

Priority weights make the PI's allocation of attention explicit. Progress and XP provide feedback on recorded completions; they are a personal motivation tool, not a measure of scientific quality.

CoordinationA chief of staff and three specialist rolesOne owner per responsibility, with the PI setting direction.

The chief of staff reports to the PI and coordinates three direct reports: research, student support, and grants and administration. The roles organize background work and bring decisions back to the PI.

Shared project memory lets each role use the same recorded context. Tasks carry an owner, next action, and state. Completion records preserve what was done and the evidence attached to it.

ContinuityKeep the record currentProject status, next actions, meetings, and follow-through survive a conversation.

Each project's context file begins with its recorded status. A status update in the monitor replaces that line, preserving the rest of the context. Task assignments and student meeting records keep commitments visible across sessions.

Information can be stale, missing, or uncertain. Those states should remain visible until the underlying record is updated. The system should not turn an old email or an absent update into a confident claim about a project's progress.

Background workPreparation that returns to a reviewable recordScheduled work supports the monitor instead of competing for attention.

Background jobs prepare daily plans, student agendas, and candidate next actions. Research and writing tools can prepare material for review. A useful output has a source, an owner, and a place in the working record.

Tealc remains the execution framework. Chat is available for requests that benefit from a conversation; the status monitor is the PI's main place to find and manage the work.

ImplementationPrivate records and a recoverable local installationLocal mutable state, backups, and explicit boundaries around public material.

The private monitor and background jobs share local records. The active database and mutable runtime memory are kept outside Drive synchronization. Backups provide a recovery path, and code changes are checked against the installation before being applied.

The public lab website serves research, teaching resources, and material approved for publication. It does not mirror the PI workspace. EvaluationDoes it improve follow-through?Useful completions and reliable records matter more than job counts.

We are evaluating whether this arrangement makes commitments easier to track, reduces repeated context gathering, and helps the PI notice work awaiting a decision. Research drafts still need scientific judgment. More automation, more tokens, or a higher XP total cannot establish that a result is correct.

The design will change as we learn where people lose their place, where records become stale, and which background outputs are useful enough to keep.
