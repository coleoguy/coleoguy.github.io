The lead · Sex chromosome evolution: maternal linkage, W degeneration and mitonuclear coevolution

Egg mimicry speeds the decay of a sex chromosome

In cuckoo finches, selection on maternally inherited egg colours is linked to W chromosome gene loss and rapid mitochondrial evolution.

Sorenson MD, Osipova E, Spottiswoode CN, Jamie GA, Dixit T, DaCosta JM, Sackton TB, Warren WC, Balakrishnan CN·Nature Ecology & Evolution, 27 Oct 2026·doi:10.1038/s41559-026-03216-w

Brood-parasitic cuckoo finches lay eggs in other birds' nests, and hosts often reject eggs that do not resemble their own. Egg colour therefore becomes a target of a coevolutionary contest. In birds, females carry ZW sex chromosomes; the female-specific W and mitochondrial DNA both pass from mothers to daughters. Michael Sorenson and colleagues compared cuckoo finches with other songbirds to ask what happens when selection repeatedly favors egg-mimicking maternal lineages. Their genomic analysis reports accelerated gene loss and pseudogene formation on the W, along with excess accumulation of mildly harmful mitochondrial changes. The proposed mechanism is genetic hitchhiking: favored maternal lineages carry linked variation with them.

Unexpectedly, two egg-colour candidates, ABCG2 and FECH, appear to have been recruited to the W while many other W genes were disappearing. The team also studied mitochondrial energy-producing protein complexes, where subunits encoded in different genomes must work together. In three-dimensional structural models, nuclear-encoded amino acids closer to mitochondrial-encoded residues changed faster, consistent with compensatory mitonuclear evolution. The result connects an ecological arms race to chromosome decay and protein evolution. But the paper's central tests rely on comparative genomic patterns and protein models, not direct assays showing that individual substitutions restore function. Historical host shifts and population structure remain alternative influences worth testing.

Where to pushCompare the W and mitochondrial genomes across host-specialist populations under models with and without host-associated population structure. Does the excess of mildly deleterious substitutions remain after accounting for demographic history? Test candidate compensatory substitutions in matched mitochondrial and nuclear protein backgrounds; spatial proximity and correlated change need not prove functional rescue.

Stalled cholesterol recycling inflames the brain

Berghoff S.A. et al. Nature Neuroscience (online 26 Oct 2026). Original paper.

Myelin is the insulating sheath around nerve fibres, and microglia are immune cells that clear its damaged fragments. Comparing acute and chronic mouse models of demyelination, Berghoff and colleagues found that chronic injury traps cholesterol in microglial lysosomes, the cell's recycling compartments. Those cells showed persistent inflammatory activation and impaired lipid handling. The team combined single-cell RNA sequencing, microscopy and lipid measurements to characterize this state. Changing sterol metabolism with an experimental compound reduced inflammation and tissue damage and improved outcomes in mouse models. Related inflammatory cells were seen in human multiple sclerosis lesions, but no treatment benefit in people was established.

Two rings circle a dead star

Gentile Fusillo N.P. et al. Nature Astronomy (online 26 Oct 2026). Original paper.

An aging Sun-like star can end as a white dwarf, surrounded by debris from planets that did not fully survive. Around WD J1328-1450, researchers used the Very Large Telescope's X-shooter instrument to detect four peaks in calcium emission lines where comparable gas discs normally show two. Their spectral model resolves the signal into two rotating rings separated by a gap. Magnesium emissions differ between rings, suggesting different source materials. The authors propose that two surviving rocky bodies maintain them, potentially one as large as Pluto, but neither body was directly imaged. The paper appeared October 26; the European Southern Observatory announced it October 27.
