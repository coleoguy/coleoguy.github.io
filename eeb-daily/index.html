The lead · Mitonuclear epistasis and genotype-by-environment interactions

Small mitochondrial differences can have big effects

Fly crosses reveal that interactions among mitochondrial genomes, nuclear genomes and environmental stress can matter more than evolutionary distance alone.

Darwin LJ, Lemieux FA, Bachtel RZ, Blocker JH, Brown CP, Griffin AD, Lerman JD, Maule OC, Raynes Y, Rand DM·Nature Communications, 14 Nov 2026·doi:10.1038/s41467-026-79307-w

Mitochondria carry small genomes, but most proteins that run their energy-producing machinery are encoded in the nucleus. A fly's physiology therefore depends on cooperation between two genetic compartments. Leah Darwin, David Rand and colleagues assembled a panel of 90 Drosophila mitochondrial-nuclear genotype combinations and measured four energy-demanding traits. Their design included mitochondrial variants sampled within species and across species. Surprisingly, interactions affecting traits were stronger for mitochondrial variation within species than for the more deeply diverged comparisons. Greater evolutionary distance did not simply translate into stronger genetic interaction effects.

The researchers then grew flies in the presence of rotenone, a chemical that inhibits mitochondrial Complex I, part of the machinery cells use to extract energy. Under that stress, phenotypes depended on interactions among mitochondrial genotype, nuclear genotype and environment. This matters because combinations of genes can perform differently as conditions change, even when their individual effects look modest. The authors interpret the pattern as compatible with stabilizing selection, which tends to preserve functioning trait combinations. That interpretation is not direct evidence of historical selective events. The experiment makes a broader point about epistasis: mitochondrial sequence divergence alone cannot tell us how strongly two genomes will interact in a living organism.

Where to pushWould the same within-species pattern survive in different nuclear backgrounds, temperatures or dietary conditions? A useful follow-up would compare replicated mitochondrial haplotypes sampled along a divergence gradient, with several nuclear genotypes and independently varied respiratory stress. That design could test whether the apparent distance paradox reflects purifying selection, environment-specific epistasis or sampling of mitochondrial variation.

Beyond EEB · A paper from outside evolution and ecology

Switching on a surviving gene copy rescues neuron defects

Chen et al., Nature Neuroscience (13 Nov 2026). Research article.

A mutation that disables one copy of a gene can leave cells with too little of its product, a condition called haploinsufficiency. Chen and colleagues tested whether activating the surviving copy could reverse developmental defects associated with two autism-linked genes, CHD8 and SCN2A. Rather than directly targeting gene promoters, they used CRISPR activation at enhancers, regulatory DNA sequences that help determine when and where genes work. In human stem-cell-derived neurons and cortical organoids, this increased expression in mutant cells and rescued several molecular and physiological abnormalities. These are experiments in cell models, not a treatment demonstrated in patients.

Beyond biology · A result from outside biology

Euclid opens a billion-object window on the cosmos

European Space Agency, 12 Nov 2026. DR1-Foundation announcement.

On 12 November, the European Space Agency released Euclid's DR1-Foundation, its first major survey dataset: more than 900 million galaxies, about 60 million quasar candidates and roughly 15,000 candidate strong gravitational lenses. A gravitational lens appears when a foreground galaxy bends light from a more distant source. The release covers about 1,900 square degrees of sky and includes spectra from over 160 million sources. Those measurements will help astronomers reconstruct cosmic structure and test ideas about dark matter and dark energy. The dataset is an observational milestone, not a completed three-dimensional map or a new measurement resolving dark energy's nature.
